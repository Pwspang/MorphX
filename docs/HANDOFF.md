## 1. Goal

Run the MorphX SOSP'26 artifact (`./run.sh`, Figures 10–16) on NVIDIA H200
(sm_90, 132 SMs, CUDA 13) instead of the original A100 (sm_80, 108 SMs, CUDA 12.4),
with **SGLang** as the LLM engine. The authors' vLLM fork isn't public (see plan §9).

## 8. Gotchas

- `run_overhead_model_part4.py` always sets `NEUTRINO_HOOK_DRIVER=<repo>/runtime/build/libcuda.so`: keep one checkout per experiment set, or two hooks load and segfault at exit.
- `./run.sh analyse-reference` rewrites files under `logs/reference/`: `git checkout logs/reference` afterwards.
- Fig 10–12 runs need ≥ 60–90 s duration (SGLang loads in ~40 s; ready barrier = duration + 5 s); multi-process schedulers need `mem=200gb`.
- SGLang's run-time JIT needs gcc ≥ 13 (`source /opt/rh/gcc-toolset-13/enable` on hpcc).
- `hf download`: one `--exclude` per pattern (`--exclude a b` treats b as a filename).
- Patched kernels on the default stream exit the process ("Default stream is not supported"), as in the original runtime.
- `pkill -f "<pattern>"` over ssh can match its own `bash -c` and kill the session; kill by PID.
- `qsub` from the checkout directory (`$PBS_O_WORKDIR` is used by several scripts).

## 9. Open questions (2026-10-26, nscc2b full re-run, gdr)

Run directory: `nscc2b:~/scratch/morphx/MorphX/logs/20261026-nscc2b` (shards per part/table,
`scripts/ae/morphx-nscc2b-shard.pbs`); finished figures are copied to `logs/20261026-nscc2b/`
locally. hpcc is no longer used.

1. **Fig 11: did the LLM's own GEMMs get confined by MorphX?** The Fig 11 MorphX env is
   `SMSCHED_TARGET_PATTERNS=half_t SMSCHED_ONLY_WHITELIST=1`. In the paper the LLM's linear
   layers were cuBLAS (vLLM fork), so only the background matmul matched. In this port SGLang's
   linear layers are mycutlass, whose 56 kernels all contain `half_t`, so the LLM's GEMMs were
   also confined to `[0, split)` and paid the patched-launch cost (~145 GEMMs/token). Observed
   (gdr, Fig 11(a)): MorphX 14.2 vs stream 9.9 ms/token at 3 mm/s (+43%); 68.7 vs 63.0 at
   30 mm/s (paper A100: 18.7 vs 20.0, and 40.0 vs 59.5). Same issue in the hpcc results.
   Fix under test: `morphx_target_stream()` + `MORPHX_TARGET_MM_STREAM=1` (only the matmul
   worker's stream is a target), runtime `runtime/build-tgt`; MorphX-only re-runs of
   Fig 11(a)/(b) (jobs 187805/187806), old logs in `prev-morphx-v1/`. Confirm, then decide.
2. **Does gdr itself cost more than memcpy on nscc2b?** MorphX's light-load overhead in Fig 11
   is ~30 µs per patched launch here vs ~8 µs on hpcc (memcpy); the microbenchmark shows gdr
   host cost 1.05 µs vs memcpy 6.46 µs, but end-to-end tiny-kernel latency only +9 µs (gdr) vs
   +11 µs (memcpy) over unhooked. Job 187714 (`scripts/baselines/n_gdr_vs_memcpy.pbs`): Fig
   11(a) MorphX with the memcpy runtime, and SGLang decode none/gdr/memcpy with
   `MORPHX_LAUNCH_STATS=1`. Suspect (unverified): the agent thread's uncached PCIe BAR reads
   interfering with kernel launches.
3. **Fig 10/12: same question for the GGNN tables.** Their MorphX env uses the default
   whitelist (`ggnn5query`), so the LLM's GEMMs aren't confined, but they are still patched and
   managed by MorphX (in the paper they were cuBLAS and untouched). Check the MorphX column when
   the shards finish; a MorphX-only re-run with `SMSCHED_ONLY_WHITELIST=1` may be needed.
4. **Where does the remaining per-launch cost go?** GDRCopy removed the host-side cost, but a
   patched launch still adds ~9–13 µs end to end (persistent agents = occupancy × 132 SMs,
   per-block atomic dispatch, SM-range checks, first-launch profiling). The A100 reference has
   the same order of overhead (Fig 15: GEMM 1.44, GEMV 1.34). Not broken down yet (proposed:
   hook without transform vs `MORPHX_AGENTS=grid` vs default on the tiny/medium kernels).
5. **H200-specific factors that may keep results from matching the paper** even after 1–3:
   H200 vs A100; Sm80-style mycutlass on Hopper; Qwen3-8B standing in for Llama-3-8B; the
   split hint scaled from the A100 (76 → 93 SMs), never re-tuned on the H200; H200 load scales
   (mm ×3, GGNN ×2, PD ×3).
6. **Fig 13/14 at high load:** with 180 s runs every method queues heavily from rps 9 up
   (TTFT in seconds to tens of seconds; hpcc used 60 s runs). MorphX is 6–17% behind stream at
   low load and runs without a split (default CI never splits on the H200; `MORPHX_CI_MODE=occ`
   variant not run yet).
7. **Fig 15 neutrino:** crashes with "illegal instruction" in its instrumented kernels on DNN
   and LLM (cells N/A).

### Answers so far (23:30 SGT)

- **Q1 (Fig 11 whitelist): confirmed, mostly fixed.** With `MORPHX_TARGET_MM_STREAM=1`
  MorphX at saturation is 15.4 ms/token vs stream 63.0 in Fig 11(a) (old: 68.7) and 17.7 vs
  21.0 in Fig 11(b) (old: 47.8), keeping 63% / 100% of stream's matmul throughput. Light load:
  12.1 vs stream 9.9 (old 14.2). **New anomaly:** in Fig 11(a) at 6 and 12 mm/s MorphX's LLM
  sits at ~25–26 ms/token for the whole run (every request), worse than at 18–30 mm/s (~15).
  Not explained yet.
- **Q2 (gdr vs memcpy): gdr is better, not the cause.** Job 187714: SGLang decode TBT none
  11.25 / gdr 11.98 / memcpy 14.56 ms; host cost per patched launch 0.91 µs (gdr) vs 5.87 µs
  (memcpy); Fig 11(a) MorphX (old whitelist) with memcpy is 15–16% worse than with gdr at every
  point (e.g. 80.2 vs 68.7 at 30 mm/s).
- **Q3 (Fig 10/12): no re-run needed.** The GGNN tables reproduce the paper's shape: at
  saturation MorphX 14.2 ms/token vs stream 53.3 (A100: 28.2 vs 116.5) with 65% of stream's
  GGNN throughput (A100: 73%); MorphX ≈ stream at light load. **New issue:** lithos breaks from
  GGNN 0.8 req/s up (LLM latency 70–3800 ms/token, throughput capped ~0.94).

- **Q6 (Fig 13, does a split help on the H200 with gdr?): no.** Profile log (job 190334):
  decode GEMMs are never CI; with the default score only the largest 4096-token prefill GEMM
  reaches the 0.9 cut-off (2048-token prompts: none), while `occ` separates prefill 0.61–1.0
  from decode 0. Forcing the split (jobs 190335 `occ` 0.6 + split 108; 190336 `occ` 0.5 +
  `SMSCHED_LLM_TRICK_MODE=16` + split 66, all 15 Fig 13 points, MorphX only) never beats stream:
  e.g. in-2048-out-256 rps 9 stream 0.068 vs MorphX 0.088 / 0.098 / 0.085 (default / occ108 /
  occ66); in-4096-out-128 rps 9 stream 0.181 vs 0.219 / 0.199 / 0.249. MorphX does beat
  baseline and chunked at in-4096-out-128 rps 7.5–9 (prefill thread). At light load MorphX is
  still +10–20% over stream (per-launch cost on ~145 LLM GEMMs per step), which a split would
  have to win back. Consistent with Track J4 on hpcc: on the H200 decode GEMMs slow down when
  they lose SMs, so the split is zero-sum. Results in `logs/20261026-nscc2b/diag-fig13-*`.

- **Split sweep (job 190793, Fig 13 in-4096-out-128 rps 6, 60 s, default CI, gdr):** MorphX
  places ~20% of its managed launches as CI on `[0, split)`, ~20% as co-runners moved to
  `[split, 132)`, ~60% on the whole GPU (69–89k managed launches per run; new
  `morphx_report()` summary). Avg latency per output token falls monotonically as the split
  widens: 66 → 0.223, 80 → 0.207, 93 → 0.190, 108 → 0.149, 120 → 0.103 s, vs stream 0.067
  (TTFT 0.18–0.23 vs 0.16). The less MorphX confines prefill, the better; even at 120 it is
  54% behind stream, so the partition only adds to the overhead here. Results in
  `logs/20261026-nscc2b/diag-fig13-split-sweep/`.
  No-split run (job 190815, same point, `MORPHX_CI_THRESHOLD=2`: 92,726 managed launches, 0
  confined, 0 moved): 0.094 s/token avg (p99 0.128, TTFT 0.171) vs stream 0.067 (0.105, 0.164).
  So at this load MorphX's overhead alone costs +40%, and the default split (108) adds another
  ~60% on top (0.149). Results in `logs/20261026-nscc2b/diag-fig13-nosplit-ci2/`.
  Two more configs (jobs 190846/190847, 60 s): in-2048-out-256 rps 9: nothing is CI (195,918
  managed launches, 0 confined, 0 moved), MorphX 0.053 vs stream 0.041 s/token (+29%, overhead
  only). in-4096-out-256 rps 6: split 80 0.128, 108 0.119, 120 0.116, no split 0.106 vs stream
  0.099 (+7%); here the splits cut TTFT (0.19–0.21 s vs 0.53 no split, 0.47 stream) at the cost
  of per-token latency. Best split for per-token latency is no split at all three configs.

- **Fig 13/14 at the paper's A100 request rates** (jobs 190734/190735, `MORPHX_PD_RPS_SCALE=1`,
  60 s, all four variants; `logs/20261027-pd-paper-rates/`): the H200 doesn't queue at these
  rates (avg 0.011–0.022 s/token for every variant, vs up to 0.27 on the A100), so all
  variants are within a few ms. MorphX beats neither stream nor baseline on any of the 30
  points: +5–15% per-token latency and +20–35 ms TTFT (e.g. 0.177 vs 0.153 s), i.e. its
  overhead with nothing to win back. Confirms the H200 needs the ×3 load to reach contention.
